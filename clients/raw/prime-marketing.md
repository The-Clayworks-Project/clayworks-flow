## 3. ICP and offer

There was never a formal ICP document. This is pieced together from the campaigns, calls and a qualification checklist in Drive.

- **Who:** companies already spending on Google, LinkedIn or Meta ads, in B2B SaaS, fintech, professional services, cybersecurity and IT services. In practice the replies came from cybersecurity, fintech and SaaS.
- **Where:** the UK, Germany and Sweden, with NL allowed. Most Apollo lists were 70% UK.
- **Size:** 11 to 500+ staff. Apollo lists used 50 to 500 staff and Seed to Series B funding.
- **Buyers:** CMO, VP or Head of Marketing, Head of Growth or Demand Gen. Founder, CEO or MD at smaller firms. They must control the budget.
- **Disqualify:** B2C, interns, companies with no budget signal, and Prime's existing clients (Econocom, Skalar, plus 18 opt-outs on the Hub blacklist).
- **Offer:** a "90-Day Paid Pipeline Sprint", live in 14 days, LinkedIn and Google only. Prime's monthly price tiers were 7.5k, 10k and 15k.
- **The guarantee never had one consistent wording:**
  - The May scripts promised money back.
  - Script v7 said "not a confirmed live offer".
  - The September and October copy said "fee returned if we miss the target".
- **Proof points:** ComplianceWise (785% ROI), Sentinels AI (+2,473% leads), DSD Europe (-21% CPL), CNC maker "Style".
- **Competitor-agency rule:** pick agencies that charge a lot and under-deliver. Never name the agencies or their review scores in the copy.

## 8. Lessons

1. **The meeting guarantee killed the account.** Promising 5 meetings a month for about €2,500 a month to a two-person agency was never achievable, and the gap was visible by July.
2. **Personalised "your competitors are ahead of you" LinkedIn outreach worked best:** a 22.4% reply rate, against about 2 to 4% for email. Follower-based lists were noisy, because agency pages draw marketers, job seekers and freelancers.
3. **Onboarding records were thin.** There was no ICP document, no stored contract, no exclusion list and no recorded kickoff. All of these should be captured in the Hub at signing.
4. **The guarantee wording drifted** across scripts and copy. One approved version should have been fixed at the start.
5. **Prospects didn't show up.** Four of the nine booked meetings didn't happen: two no-shows and two never rescheduled. Confirmation calls and reminders would have helped.
6. **Billing ended in a dispute** because the notice terms were verbal. Put them in writing.
