# ServiceFirst: next campaign plan (research)

**Prepared:** 3 Nov 2026, for Joy (Clayworks). Research only: nothing was sent, built, uploaded or enriched.
**Data pulled:** SmartLead analytics and every reply thread for B1 to B7 (3 Nov), Client Hub meetings and call activity (ServiceFirst tenant), Fireflies client calls (27 Aug, 10 Sep, 1 Oct, 16 Oct, 23 Oct) and internal syncs to 2 Nov, the ServiceFirst context file and plan PDFs, plus web research (sources at the end).

---

## 0. The short version

- **The program has produced one real positive reply in two months.** About 5,000 people emailed, 133 replies, and 93 of those are out-of-office. The only "send it over" came from Riteway Transport's VP Operations on 9 Sep (B1). Client Hub shows **one meeting ever** (General Assembly, education, marked not qualified) and **1,396 dials** with only 17 calls longer than a minute.
- **Copy is not the main problem.** It has been rewritten three times. The root causes are: (1) we email people who are not in the market (no buying trigger), (2) the offer is a price discount ("setup fee waived", "half the cost") on a decision nobody has made yet, (3) the lists are stale (22 of the 40 non-OOO replies say the person left), and (4) calling, the one channel that books BPO meetings, barely connects because we have few direct numbers and dial each person only about twice.
- **Timing is against us for US cold outreach until early January.** ServiceFirst (Ierah) and Theo both said on 23 Oct that US buyers are already pushing to January. Thanksgiving is 26 Nov.
- **The breakthrough plan:** use the next three weeks to book meetings *for January*, not to sell now. Ierah is planning a US trip in Jan/Feb (SF and New York, wherever demand is). That trip is real scarcity and it is exactly what she says buyers want: face to face. Pair it with a much stronger, risk-reversed offer: a small **side-by-side pilot** (3 seats, from the 3 FTE floor Ierah accepted on 23 Oct) that the buyer can walk away from if it doesn't beat what they have. Aim it only at companies that **already outsource or are visibly straining**, in the verticals where ServiceFirst already wins (lending and financial ops, property management, ecommerce).
- **Ranked concepts:** (1) "Coffee in January" to in-market US accounts, launch now on the one free mailbox, call-led; (2) "Side-by-side pilot" switch campaign for companies that already use a BPO, launch 5 Jan on B2's four mailboxes; (3) Australia, rebuilt around signals and phones, run until ~18 Dec and restart mid-Jan.

### 1.2 Calls (Client Hub, CloudTalk activity, 10 Sep to 29 Oct)
- 1,396 call activities; 765 with duration data across 605 contacts (about **1.3 dials per contact**).
- 82 calls lasted 30 seconds or more, **17 lasted 60 seconds or more**, 5 lasted 90 seconds or more. None has a note or disposition logged.
- Several of the longest calls were with people outside the ICP: a town purchasing agent, a deputy fire chief, a "Chief Heart Officer", a talent management partner. That's both a list problem and a title-filter problem.
- Benchmarks put cold-call connect rates at about 2 to 6% per dial, and top teams at 8 to 12% (Everything-PR, Prospeo, 2026). With about 1.3 dials per person and mostly switchboard numbers, we're far below the 6 to 8 attempts a contact needs.

### 1.3 Root causes, ranked

1. **No buying trigger (ICP plus timing).** Outsourcing a CX team is a rare, high-trust decision. At any moment only a small share of even a perfect-fit list is evaluating. Our lists are "right title, right industry", not "in the market now". White Whale's own audit (SF_signal_types, 23 Oct) shows most automated signals are noise: New Funding 0 of 42 relevant, International Support Ops 0 of 57, BPO Renewal Window 0 of 8. The signals that *did* hold up were **New CFO (13 of 15 relevant), New COO (7 of 11) and Hiring CS Process Builder (3 of 4)**.
2. **The offer is a discount, not a reason to act.** "Setup fee waived" and "about half the cost" only matter to someone who has already decided to outsource. To everyone else, it reads like every other PH BPO email. ServiceFirst's real advantage (100% on-site, automated QA, lending-ops experience) is stated but never *shown*, and there's no risk reversal. Hormozi's value equation says the strongest levers are likelihood of success and less effort/risk, not price.
3. **List quality and decay.** About 5% bounce overall (7% on B6 and B4), and 22 left-company replies. Unvalidated Blitz/Apollo data plus a three-month-old B1 scrape. Only B7 was ZeroBounce-validated, and it's the only one with a clean bounce rate.
4. **Calling is under-powered.** Calls book most meetings at Clayworks (KB section 7), and BDRs say the competitor list is the best to call (23 Oct). But few direct or mobile numbers (B7: 127 phones for 1,661 leads), 1.3 dials per contact, and no logged outcomes mean the phone channel has had no fair test.
5. **Timing.** B4 landed after holiday hiring decisions. Now the US is entering the Thanksgiving-to-New-Year slowdown (Ierah and Theo, 23 Oct). Australia closes from about mid-December to mid-January.
6. **Deliverability and rule drift.** The servicefirstdirect / askservicefirst / servicefirstseats / servicefirstcontact mailboxes were at 41 to 63% reputation in late Sept, and one Gmail domain was blacklisted (23 Oct talking points). B6 and B7 copy also breaks house rules: "Most teams we talk to" (banned 30 Oct), "Worth a quick look?", a "quick one for..." subject, "free setup for..." subject lines, and B6 step 3 has a blank subject, so it threads as a reply. Fix these on not-started leads, but none of them explains zero positives on their own.

**Bottom line:** another copy rewrite on the same lists won't get the breakthrough. We need a smaller list of companies that are in the market now, an offer that removes the risk of trying ServiceFirst, real phone coverage, and a timing hook that works across the holidays.

- Any title outside ops, CX, finance ops or the exec team (purchasing agents at towns, HR, talent, L&D, marketing).

## 4. The offer (Hormozi value equation)

The current offer improves only *price*. The next one should push up **likelihood** (proof) and push down **risk, time and effort**.

**Proposed core offer: "Try a small team side by side."**
> Start with 3 seats (or one shift) working next to your current team or provider for 60 days. We hire, train and run them from our secure Manila office, and we waive the setup fee. If the numbers (QA score, response time, CSAT) don't beat what you have today, you walk away.

| Value lever | What we add | Needs client approval? |
|---|---|---|
| Dream outcome | "A trained team clearing your queue by [date]" in their function (loan servicing, resident billing, ecommerce support) | No |
| Likelihood | A **3-minute video walk of the Manila floor** (proves on-site, no WFH) and an anonymised lending-ops result (e.g. "a US consumer lender's servicing queue, QA from X to Y") | **Yes**: the video and any anonymised stats |
| Time delay | 30-day target to go live (a target, never a guarantee) | Already approved |
| Effort and sacrifice | We write the job specs, hire, train and QA. They send their SOPs and get a one-page team plan for their company. | No |
| **Risk reversal** | 3-seat, 60-day pilot with a walk-away clause; optional: replace any agent who isn't working within 14 days at no cost; optional: SLA credits in the pilot (Deloitte notes 12 to 15% of monthly fees are commonly put at risk) | **Yes**: pilot length, minimum seats, walk-away terms, replacement promise. They have said "no free months", so don't offer free time. |
| Real scarcity | **Ierah's January US trip**: limited in-person slots in New York and SF | **Yes**: dates, cities, number of slots |

Keep "setup fee waived" as a sweetener, not the headline. Keep cost language out of touch 1 (client rule) and use "more trained people for the same budget" later.

**Be careful with the Coinbase/TaskUs story.** Court filings allege office-based agents in India were bribed (about 69,000 users affected). It shows offshore data risk in general, but it was not a work-from-home failure, so don't use it as proof that on-site is safer.

## 7. Recommended campaigns (ranked)

Must have at least one trigger from 3.2 (already outsources, new COO/VP CX in 90 days, 5+ ageing support/servicing roles, or a complaint spike).
- **List size:** about **120 to 150 accounts, 2 contacts each (about 250 to 300 people)**. That fits one mailbox at about 20 to 25 new per day over 13 business days. *Estimate; confirm with Blitz job and company counts.*
- **Also include (zero new data):** re-engage Riteway (Joshua Bailey, VP Ops, direct line in his signature) and the warmest B1 and B5 contacts by phone, offering the January meeting.
- **Mailbox:** ierahjaochico@servicefirstdirect.com (only free one; check reputation first).
- **Offer:** coffee with Ierah in January, or a 15-minute video intro now. Floor video plus a one-page team plan. Pilot offer in touch 3.
- **Email outline (draft; needs client approval of trip details):**
  1. Subject `{{company}} in january`: "Hi {{first_name}}, Ierah from our team is in {{city}} in late January, meeting ops leaders at {{peer_group}}. I saw {{signal_line}}. Usually that means the queue grows faster than you can hire and train. Is that how it feels at {{company}}? We run trained, on-site teams in Manila, never work from home. Could Ierah buy you a coffee while she's there?"
  2. Subject `our manila floor`: "Hi {{first_name}}, a lot of providers have agents working from home. I'd rather show you ours than tell you: a 3-minute video of our Manila floor, plus a one-page team plan for {{company}} (team size, shifts, start date). Want me to send both?"
  3. Subject `three seats, side by side`: "Hi {{first_name}}, here's an easy way to test us. We start 3 people next to your current team or provider for 60 days. We hire, train and run them, and we waive the setup fee. If the numbers don't beat what you have, you walk away. Is that worth 15 minutes before the holidays, or early January?"
